Reading the numbers

What the terms on this page mean

Tax charged in the accounts and tax paid in cash

A group's accounts show tax in two different ways, and the two can differ a lot in any one year.

Tax charged in the accountsThe tax that belongs to this year's profit, whether the group pays it now or in later years. It is the figure used to work out a group's tax rate.
Tax paid in cashThe money that actually left the group for tax authorities during the year. It can include tax on earlier years' profits and instalments of older bills, minus any refunds.

Pfizer is an example. Its tax charge for 2025 was a credit of $0.3bn, because of tax it expects to save in later years, but it paid $4.7bn in cash. The Ireland chart uses tax paid in cash, because that is what groups now report by country.

Pre-tax profit

What a group earned after its costs, before income tax. Groups split it between profit made by their US companies and profit made by their companies abroad.

Where profit is reported

A multinational is a group of many companies. Profit is counted in the country of the company that records it, which often depends on which company owns patents or other rights. Sales are counted where the customers are.

The 5% rule

For financial years starting in 2025 or later, US accounting rules (known as ASU 2023-09) require groups to name any country that receives 5% or more of the income tax they paid in cash. Countries below that line are grouped together as "other".

A US loss

Some groups report a loss in the United States while making a large profit abroad. The group as a whole is still profitable. In the chart below, this is marked with an arrow.

About this page

Sources, method and updates

All figures come from each group's latest annual report (Form 10-K) filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. We took them from the income tax note and the note on sales by country. Amounts are in US dollars. Each group's card links to the tables we used.

We started with 16 large US pharma and tech multinationals, two industries with major operations in Ireland.

We will update this page as new annual reports come out. Apple's next report, expected around the end of October 2026, should be the first to show its tax paid by country. Reports for 2026 from the other groups, whose years end in December, follow from February 2027. We also plan to add more groups.
